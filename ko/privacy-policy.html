Kroky

Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 25, 2026

This is a translation. If it differs from the English version, the English version applies.

Kroky: Walk Breaks ("Kroky") is an iPhone app that suggests walks. This policy explains, in plain language, what the app uses, where it goes and what stays on your iPhone. The short version: we don't collect anything. Kroky has no server, no account and no analytics. The few things that leave your iPhone go to Apple or to OpenStreetMap so the app can find parks, plan a route and check the weather.

Who we are

Kroky is made by nakolini, a studio of Dmytro Hryb and Vitalii Kikot ("we"). You can reach us at support@kroky.nakolini.com.

What we collect

Nothing. We don't receive your steps, your location, your calendar or anything else. There is no login and no server we run. If you email us, we receive your email and use it only to answer you.

Steps from Apple Health

Kroky reads your step count, and your walking step length to turn steps into distance, from Apple Health, to know when you've been sitting and how many steps are left today. It only reads Health data; it never writes to Health. Your Health data is read and used on your iPhone and is never sent anywhere, unless you export your walks from a test version yourself (see below). It is never used for advertising or shared with anyone.

Location

Kroky asks for location only while you're using the app, never "Always". It gets one location fix when you plan a walk and when Home shows the weather, to plan a loop near you and to get the weather forecast.

During a walk you start, Kroky follows your location to show where you are on the route, including while the screen is locked; iOS shows the location indicator while it does. It stops when the walk ends. The route you walked is kept only on your iPhone, in your walk history, until you delete that walk. Kroky never tracks you outside a walk you started.

Kroky saves the last location on your iPhone, rounded to two decimal places (about 1 km), so it can check the weather for walk reminders without asking for your location again.

Test versions of Kroky (TestFlight) can export the walk history as a file, to help improve the app. The file holds each walk's precise route and times, its step counts from Apple Health (including how many came from your iPhone and how many from your Apple Watch), the app version and time zone it was walked with, whether Precise Location was on, and the voice settings and spoken directions used on the walk. It names no one: the only device details are the iPhone model and iOS version. It is made on your iPhone and leaves it only if you choose to share it. The App Store version has no export.

What leaves your iPhone, and to whom

  • Apple Weather (WeatherKit) receives a location (your current one on Home, or the rounded saved one for reminders) to return the hourly forecast. This goes through Apple and is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Apple Maps (MapKit) receives the start and turning points of your walk to plan walking routes, and the area around you to find parks the walk goes through. During a walk, it also receives your current location when Kroky plans the way to the start, back to the route or home. This goes through Apple.
  • OpenStreetMap's Overpass API (overpass-api.de, or the public mirror overpass.private.coffee when it's busy) receives the area to search for parks, main roads and footpaths, so loops can keep off busy streets: an approximate starting point, rounded to about 100 m, and a search radius. No account, device identifier or other personal data is sent with it. The results are cached on your iPhone for a month (and used for up to six months if Overpass can't be reached), so the same area isn't asked for again.
  • Google Maps or Apple Maps receives the loop only if you choose Open in Google Maps or Open in Apple Maps; Go follows the walk in Kroky itself. That app's own privacy policy applies from then on.

Calendar

Only if you turn on Skip my meetings, Kroky reads your calendar on your iPhone to find the times you're busy, so it doesn't nudge you during meetings. It uses only the start and end times and whether you're marked busy. Your calendar is never sent anywhere.

Notifications

Walk nudges are local notifications scheduled on your iPhone. Kroky doesn't use a push server.

Purchases

Kroky Pro is sold as a yearly subscription or a one-time lifetime purchase, both handled by Apple's App Store. Apple handles your payment details and billing, and they never reach us. The app only sees your entitlement: which plan you have and, for the subscription, when it renews or ends. Kroky checks it with Apple and remembers it on your iPhone.

What stays on your iPhone, and for how long

Your settings, step goal, nudge hours, the loops you were offered and how you rated them, your walks, the places you asked Kroky to avoid and the cached map data are stored only on your iPhone. They stay until you delete them in the app or delete the app, which deletes them all. They are included in your iPhone's own backups, which Apple handles.

Your choices

Every permission can be changed at any time: Health access in the Health app (your profile › Apps › Kroky), location, notifications and calendar in the iOS Settings app › Kroky. Kroky keeps working without each of them, with less to offer. Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to show, correct or delete; the data on your iPhone is yours to delete. If you have a question about your rights under data protection law, such as the GDPR, write to us.

No analytics, no tracking

Kroky has no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking SDKs, no account system and no third-party code beyond Apple's own frameworks. We don't track you across apps or websites, and we don't sell or share any data.

This website

This website has no cookies, no analytics and no tracking. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may keep technical logs such as IP addresses to keep the service secure; see GitHub's Privacy Statement.

Children

Kroky isn't directed at children under 13, and it doesn't collect personal information from anyone.
